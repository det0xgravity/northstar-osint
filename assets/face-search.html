Face search engines

northstar · read this before you upload anyone's face

Stop if any of these apply.
  • The person may be under 18.
  • The photo shows a victim (of a crime, abuse or exploitation), or it came from a victim or their family.
  • You have no lawful basis to process this person's biometric data, or your organisation's policy forbids it.
  • You're trying to identify someone out of curiosity, or for anyone outside an authorised case.
What you're about to do.
  • Your photo leaves this computer. These are commercial services run by third parties. They may keep, index or analyse what you upload, and some are in other jurisdictions.
  • A face is biometric data. Many laws treat it as specially protected (for example the GDPR's special-category data, or Illinois' BIPA). Some services' terms only allow searching for your own face: check them.
  • Results are look-alikes, not identifications. False matches are common, especially with small, blurred, angled, old or low-light photos, and accuracy differs between demographic groups. A match is a lead to corroborate independently, never proof of who someone is.
  • Some results are sensitive: adult sites, mugshots or offender registries. Treat them with care, and don't repeat them outside the case.
  • Record why. Use northstar → Image analysis → Face search engines instead of these links: it asks for your reason and writes it to the case notes and audit log. Opening them from here records nothing.
I've read this, and the search is lawful and authorised. Show the engines.
  • PimEyes: faces on public web pages (paid for full results).
  • FaceCheck.ID: faces on social media, news and other sites, including mugshots.
  • Search4faces: profile photos on Russian-language social networks (VK, Odnoklassniki) and others.
  • Lenso.ai: reverse image search with a separate face category.

For photos of places and objects, use the ordinary reverse image search engines instead (TinEye, Google Lens, Bing, Yandex), or Image analysis in northstar.
